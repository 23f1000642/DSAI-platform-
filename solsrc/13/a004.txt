=== 13.4.21

=== 13.5.2
Each probe narrows the range the key must lie in. A sequence is possible only if every later value stays inside the current (lower, upper) bounds.

| seq | trace of bounds | possible? |
|---|---|---|
| (a) 61 52 14 17 40 43 | <61, <52, >14, >17, >40, then 43 | ✔ |
| (b) 2 3 50 40 60 43 | >2, >3, <50, >40, then **60** is outside (40, 50) | ✘ |
| (c) 10 65 31 48 37 43 | >10, <65, >31, <48, >37, then 43 | ✔ |
| (d) 81 61 52 14 41 43 | <81, <61, <52, >14, >41, then 43 | ✔ |
| (e) 17 77 27 66 18 43 | >17, <77, >27, <66, then **18** is outside (27, 66) | ✘ |

**(a), (c) and (d)** are possible. **(b) and (e)** are not.
=== 13.5.3
In a BST preorder, after the root, all the smaller keys (left subtree) must come before all the larger keys. The same rule applies recursively.

- **D: 5 | 3 1 2 4 | 7 6 8.** Left: 3, then {1, 2} and {4}. Right: 7, then {6} and {8}. ✔
- A: right part 7 8 6 lists 8 before 6, but 6 < 7 belongs in 7's left subtree. ✘
- B: 6 appears inside the left part, before 4. ✘
- C: in 3's subtree, 1 comes after 4. ✘

**Option D**.
