=== 9.1.1
Use Bayes' theorem. For each box, multiply the probability of choosing it by the probability of drawing white from it:

- Box 1: $\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac13 = \tfrac16$
- Box 2: $\tfrac16\cdot\tfrac23 = \tfrac19$
- Box 3: $\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac12 = \tfrac16$

$P(\text{white}) = \tfrac16 + \tfrac19 + \tfrac16 = \tfrac49$, so
$$P(\text{Box 2}\mid\text{white}) = \frac{1/9}{4/9} = \mathbf{0.25}$$
=== 9.2.1
The factorisation is the Bayesian network U → W ← V, W → X and W → Y.

- **A ✔.** Y depends on V only through W.
- **B ✔.** X depends on U only through W.
- **D ✔.** X and Y are both children of W, and conditionally independent given it.
- **C ✘.** U and V are independent on their own, but conditioning on their common child W makes them **dependent** ("explaining away", a v-structure).

**Option C**.
=== 9.2.2
Use the network's factorisation:
$$P(U{=}1)\,P(V{=}1\mid U{=}1)\,P(W{=}1\mid U{=}1)\,P(Z{=}1\mid V{=}1,W{=}1) = 0.5\times0.5\times1\times0.5 = \mathbf{0.125}$$
=== 9.3.1
