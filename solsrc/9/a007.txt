=== 9.23.1
Square-invariant means $\{\alpha^2, \beta^2\} = \{\alpha, \beta\}$ as multisets.

- **Case $\alpha^2 = \alpha$, $\beta^2 = \beta$:** both are in {0, 1}, giving the polynomials (0,0), (1,1) and (0,1).
- **Case $\alpha^2 = \beta$, $\beta^2 = \alpha$:** then $\alpha^4 = \alpha$, so α = 0, α = 1 or α is a primitive cube root of unity ω. α = ω gives β = ω², which is a new polynomial, $(x-\omega)(x-\omega^2)$.

The distinct polynomials are $x^2$, $(x-1)^2$, $x(x-1)$ and $x^2 + x + 1$. Of these 4, two have equal roots: $2/4 = \mathbf{0.5}$.
