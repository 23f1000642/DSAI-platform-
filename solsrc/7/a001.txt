=== 7.0.1
a: 12
Let the rank be $r$. Then the nullity is $n - r = 6$.

$M^2 = 0$ means every column of $M$ lies in the null space of $M$. So $\text{col}(M)\subseteq\text{null}(M)$, which gives $r\le n-r = 6$.

So $n = r + 6 \le 12$. This is achievable: take $M = \begin{pmatrix}0&I_6\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, which has rank 6 and nullity 6. The maximum is **n = 12**.

=== 7.1.2
In a Boolean product, row $i$ of the result is the OR of the rows of B selected by the 1s in row $i$ of A.

| A's row | selects B's rows | result |
|---|---|---|
| [1 1 0] | rows 1, 2 | [1 1 1 0] |
| [0 1 0] | row 2 | [0 1 1 0] |
| [1 1 0] | rows 1, 2 | [1 1 1 0] |
| [0 0 1] | row 3 | [1 0 1 1] |

**Option A**.
=== 7.2.1
Sort the points by x-coordinate. Take any three points $P_1, P_2, P_3$ in x-order. The slope of $P_1P_3$ is a weighted average of the slopes of $P_1P_2$ and $P_2P_3$, so it can't be steeper than both.

So the steepest line always joins two points that are **adjacent in x-order**. **Option A**.
