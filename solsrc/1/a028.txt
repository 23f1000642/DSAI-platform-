=== 1.61.7
**(a)** Every element is smaller than the elements to its right and below it, so the minimum is at the top-left: **i = 1, j = 1**.

**(b)** Put $x$ at $A[1][1]$ and sift it down. At each step, swap with the **smaller** of the cell below and the cell to the right, until $x$ is smaller than both.
```
procedure insert (x: integer);
var i,j: integer;
begin
   i:=1; j:=1; A[i][j]:=x;
   while (x > A[i+1][j] or x > A[i][j+1]) do
      if A[i+1][j] < A[i][j+1] then begin
         A[i][j]:=A[i+1][j]; i:=i+1;
      end
      else begin
         A[i][j]:=A[i][j+1]; j:=j+1;
      end;
   A[i][j]:=x
end
```
$x$ is smaller than everything in the last row and column, so the loop stops before running off the array. Each step increases $i+j$ by one, so there are at most $2n$ steps: **$O(n)$**.

=== 1.61.10
Rows and columns are both sorted, so:

- **1st smallest** = $A[1][1]$, with no comparisons needed.
- **2nd smallest** is one of $A[1][2]$ or $A[2][1]$, the only cells with no other unseen cell above or to the left.
- **3rd smallest** is the winner of a comparison between the other candidate and the new cell exposed by removing the 2nd.

The decision tree:
```
              A[1][2] < A[2][1] ?
             /                     \
          yes                       no
   2nd = A[1][2]               2nd = A[2][1]
   A[1][3] < A[2][1] ?         A[3][1] < A[1][2] ?
     /         \                 /         \
 3rd=A[1][3]  3rd=A[2][1]   3rd=A[3][1]  3rd=A[1][2]
```
($A[2][2]$ can never be 3rd, because it's larger than both $A[1][2]$ and $A[2][1]$.)

It takes **2 comparisons**, which is the minimum possible.
