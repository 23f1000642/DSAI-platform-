=== 1.42.25
Name the vertices T (top), L, R, BL and BR. The edges are T–BL 1, T–BR 3, T–L 4, L–BL 4, BL–BR 4, BR–R 5 and T–R $x$.

The MST always takes T–BL (1) and T–BR (3). BL–BR (4) would close a cycle with them, so it's out. That leaves:

- **L:** joined by T–L or L–BL, both weight 4. That's 2 choices.
- **R:** joined by BR–R (5) or T–R ($x$). Setting $x = 5$ makes them a tie, giving 2 choices.

$x = 5$ gives the most MSTs: $2\times2 = \mathbf{4}$.

=== 1.42.31
$A[i][j] = 1$ for $j \le i$, so the edges go from each vertex to **smaller-numbered** vertices (ignoring self-loops).

A spanning arborescence rooted at 5 gives every other vertex one parent, and that parent must have a larger number:

- vertex 4: parent 5 (1 choice)
- vertex 3: parent 4 or 5 (2 choices)
- vertex 2: 3 choices
- vertex 1: 4 choices

Parents are always larger, so no cycle is possible, and every combination is a valid tree.

The count is $1\times2\times3\times4 = \mathbf{24}$.

=== 1.42.34
