=== 3.20.60
- **Determinants**: the group of attributes on the left side of an FD → (d)
- **Candidate key**: uniquely identifies a row → (b)
- **Non-redundancy** (minimality of a key): no attribute can be added or removed without losing that property → (a)
- **Functional dependency**: a constraint between two sets of attributes → (c)

1-d, 2-b, 3-a, 4-c, **option A**.
=== 3.20.61
"No partial functional dependencies (of non-prime attributes on a key)" is exactly the definition of **2NF**. **Option B**.
=== 3.20.62
a: C
A decomposition preserves dependencies if every FD in F either appears directly in some $R_i$ **or** can be inferred from the union of the FDs that hold on the individual $R_i$, i.e. $(\bigcup F_i)^+ = F^+$. **Both**, **option C**.
=== 3.20.63
BCNF ⊂ 3NF, so a relation in BCNF is always in 3NF. **Option A**.
=== 3.20.64
3NF removes **transitive dependencies** of non-prime attributes on the key. **Option B**.
=== 3.20.65
- **Decomposition:** $X\to YZ$ gives $X\to Y$ and $X\to Z$ → iv
- **Union:** $X\to Y$ and $X\to Z$ give $X\to YZ$ → iii
- **Composition:** $X\to Y$ and $Z\to W$ give $XZ\to YW$ → i
- **Pseudo-transitivity:** $X\to Y$ and $YW\to Z$ give $XW\to Z$ → ii

a-iv, b-iii, c-i, d-ii, **option D**.
=== 3.20.66

The official key is **A**, but the statement is muddled. If V_name depends on V_no, which is **part** of the composite key (V_no, V_ord_no), that's a **partial** dependency, and it would **violate** 2NF.
