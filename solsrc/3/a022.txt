=== 3.51.15
The join pairs each girl with every boy who has marks **≥** hers. The projection gives the girls for whom such a boy exists. Subtracting them from all girls leaves the girls whose marks are **greater than every boy's**. **Option D**.
=== 3.51.16
$\Pi_{studId}(\text{females})\times\Pi_{courseId}(enroll)$ is every (female, course) pair. Subtracting `enroll` leaves the pairs where that female did **not** take that course. Projecting courseId gives the courses that **at least one female didn't take**, i.e. courses where only a proper subset of the female students enrolled. **Option B**.
=== 3.51.17
- **I:** the P values of (P,Q) keys that appear in **both** R and S.
- **III:** $\Pi_{P,Q}(R)\cap\Pi_{P,Q}(S)$, then project P. Same as I.

=== 3.51.18
Two selections in a row combine into one selection on $F_1\wedge F_2$. And since $A_1\subset A_2$, projecting $A_2$ then $A_1$ is the same as projecting $A_1$ directly:
$$\pi_{A1}(\sigma_{F1\wedge F2}(r))$$
**Option A**.
=== 3.51.19

=== 3.51.22

=== 3.51.23
**First part:** $\sigma_{P.Y=R.Y\wedge R.V=V2}(P\times R)$. The R tuples with V2 are (Y3, V2) and (Y2, V2). P has no Y3, and for Y2 it has (X2, Y2, Z2). So the result is **{X2}**.

**Second part:** $\sigma_{Q.Y=R.Y\wedge Q.T>2}(Q\times R)$. The Q tuples with T > 2 are (X1, Y2, 5) and (X1, Y1, 6). Y2 and Y1 both appear in R. So the result is **{X1}**.

$\{X2\} - \{X1\} = \{X2\}$, which is **1** tuple.
